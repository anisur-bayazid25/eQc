# Starter codebook — fictional climate resilience study

Version: training draft 1. Codes describe participants' scripted accounts; they do not establish climate causation. Root themes and child codes may both be applied when appropriate. Three coders share these definitions but include deliberate interpretation differences.

## Environmental disruption

Describe exposure, disruption and the aftermath of hazardous weather; distinguish the reported event from any scientific attribution.

### Flooding and storm impacts

Water, wind or debris disrupting homes and routes. Exclude metaphorical uses of flooding.

### Slope and access disruption

Landslide concerns, unstable ground or blocked hillside routes.

### Continuing risk and worry

Uncertainty or vigilance continuing after an event; exclude a warning message alone.

## Livelihoods and recovery costs

Changes to earnings, expenses, borrowing and the capacity to restart work.

### Income interruption

Missed sales, fishing, farming or paid work. Include forgone income without physical damage.

### Debt and recurring costs

Borrowing, repayments, rent and costs that continue during disruption.

### Restarting work

Resources or relationships required to resume a livelihood. Distinguish from the initial loss.

## Essential services and care

Water, health, schooling and household care burdens that shape recovery.

### Water access and safety

Availability, contamination, transport, storage and time required for drinking water.

### Unpaid care and time

Care, cleaning or domestic work and its opportunity costs. Income loss alone is insufficient.

### Access to health and school

Practical routes and barriers to clinics, schools or accessible shelter facilities.

## Preparedness and mutual support

Warnings, evacuation, local help and household preparation.

### Actionable warnings

How information is delivered, trusted and translated into a decision.

### Evacuation constraints

Deciding, leaving, transport, medicines, privacy and temporary accommodation.

### Mutual aid and inclusion

Informal shared food, tools, transport or labour, including exclusion from networks.

## Institutions and fair support

Public support, rules, voice, housing rights and the limits of household adaptation.

### Participation and voice

Residents influencing priorities or being excluded by meeting arrangements.

### Eligibility and administration

Documentation, ownership, tenant status or procedures governing access to support.

### Limits of household adaptation

Changes beyond household resources or authority, including infrastructure and affordable technical advice.

## Place, mobility and future

Attachment, decisions to stay or move and aspirations for a liveable future.

### Place and social ties

Identity, relationships, customers or land that give a place value.

### Considering mobility

Temporary or permanent movement, its tradeoffs and family negotiation.

### Future aspirations

Hopes for education, choice, rest, safety or fair future conditions.

## Coding rules

Code resident speech, not interviewer questions or the fictional-data header. Retain enough context to interpret the statement. Multiple codes may overlap. Apply Mobility when movement is considered, even without a final decision. Apply Unpaid care only when care/cleaning/time is explicit; income loss can be an additional code. Distinguish participation in decisions from eligibility for assistance. Do not interpret repeated coding by three coders as three independent interviewees.
