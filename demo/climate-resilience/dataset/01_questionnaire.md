# Fictional interview questionnaire

Purpose: explore how environmental disruption affects work, services, care, support and mobility. All material is invented for eQc testing and video demonstration. Estimated real-world interview length would be 30–45 minutes; these scripts are shorter.

## Opening script

For a real study, explain purpose, voluntary participation, recording, confidentiality, skipping questions and stopping; obtain appropriate consent before interviewing. This dummy package has no real consent or participants. Use participant aliases and avoid collecting precise home locations.

## Q1. Everyday life

Please tell me about your household and an ordinary day here.

Optional probes: Who depends on your work? What makes this place important to you?

## Q2. A difficult event

Describe a recent weather or environmental event that disrupted your life.

Optional probes: What happened first? What did you notice, and what happened afterwards?

## Q3. Water and essential services

How did the event affect drinking water, sanitation, electricity or access to care?

Optional probes: Who collected water? What changed in the time or cost involved?

## Q4. Work and household money

What changed in your work, food supply, income or debts?

Optional probes: How did you respond? Were there costs that other people did not see?

## Q5. Warnings and information

How do you hear about danger, and how do you decide which information to trust?

Optional probes: Did the message reach everyone in time? Was it understandable and useful?

## Q6. Safety and evacuation

Tell me about deciding whether to leave or stay during an emergency.

Optional probes: What made leaving easier or harder? Who needed additional help?

## Q7. Mutual support

Who helped, and what did neighbours or community groups do?

Optional probes: Who could contribute? Did anyone fall outside those networks?

## Q8. Unequal burdens

Did the disruption affect some people more than others?

Optional probes: Think about care responsibilities, disability, age, renting and insecure work.

## Q9. Institutions and voice

What support did you receive, and how much say did residents have in decisions?

Optional probes: What worked? What barriers or eligibility rules mattered?

## Q10. Changes and adaptation

What have you changed to prepare for the next disruption?

Optional probes: What seems useful? What is costly, uncertain or difficult to maintain?

## Q11. Staying or moving

Have you considered moving away, temporarily or permanently?

Optional probes: What would you gain or lose? Who would make that decision?

## Q12. Future priorities

If residents could choose three changes, what would you prioritise?

Optional probes: What would fair support look like? Is there anything I have missed?

## Closing

Thank the participant and ask whether anything should be corrected or excluded. In this fictional exercise, discuss how interviewing choices influence later coding.
